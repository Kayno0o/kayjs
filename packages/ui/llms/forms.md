# kayjs-ui: Inputs, selects, editors and forms

## Common tasks

### TextInput, Textarea, Select

- One convention for every field: `onInput(value)` is the live value, on each edit, and `onChange(value)` the committed one (blur or Enter for text, the pick for a choice). A choice control (Select, RadioGroup, Segmented, Combobox, IconPicker) passes the picked option as a second argument, `onChange(value, option)`, and ChipGroup and MultiSelect `onChange(values, options)`. For DateInput and TimeInput `onChange` is the whole value (deduped), not blur. Rating reports only its number. TextInput, Textarea, MarkdownEditor (`onChange` once left with changed text), DateInput, TimeInput and ColorInput (shades dragged over) and Slider have both. Tabs passes the tab as `onChange(value, tab)`.
- `TextInput(value={title()} onInput={title.set} onChange={save})`:
  - `onInput(value)` runs on each keystroke, and `onChange(value)` once the value is committed, on blur or Enter. `onBlur(event)` gets the box's own blur, as on an `input`.
  - `icon` draws an icon inside the box at its start, the text beginning after it and screen readers skipping it: `TextInput(icon="search")`, an `IconSlot` name or an imported icon, as Button and EmptyState take it. A `type="search"` box draws the `search` icon without it, and `icon={false}` leaves it out. The icon is the `icon` part (`class={{ icon: 'text-accent' }}`), and while one is drawn the box sits in a `field` part. Combobox takes `icon` the same way; Textarea and Select draw none.
  - `type="number"` hands back a number, or `null` once emptied, to both callbacks, and takes a number, `null` or a string as `value`: `TextInput(type="number" value={count()} onInput={count.set})` with `signal<number | null>(1)`. A box partway through a number, such as `1,`, reports nothing until it parses. A parent writing back its own spelling of the same number leaves the caret alone.
  - Without `value` and `onInput` it is a plain field keeping what is typed, for a form posting to an action without JavaScript: `TextInput(name="email" type="email" required autocomplete="email")`, with `value` to start it filled. Inside a form posting to an action it takes what a refused post sent in place of `value`, as every field control does (Select, RadioGroup, Segmented, ChipGroup, MultiSelect, Checkbox, Toggle, DateTimeInput, DateRangeInput, Combobox, RowsField and IconPicker among them) when it has no callback of its own; RangeInput posts no name, so it is not refilled. A field named like a secret is never refilled, and forms posting to one action share one refused post.
- `value` is optional on every field control, and a control without it keeps what is picked and posts it under its `name` with no JavaScript, a form reset going back to what it first showed: TextInput, Textarea and the pickers start empty, Select on its first option (a Select or Segmented holding a `param` starts on the option the address names, else `paramDefault`), RadioGroup and Segmented on their first option that is not disabled (`value={null}` starts with none), Slider at `min`, the first option and `min` being read once as the control is drawn, ColorInput black, Rating at 0 and ChipGroup with no chip ticked.
- Every field control (TextInput, Textarea, Select, Combobox, MultiSelect, DateInput, DateRangeInput, DateTimeInput, TimeInput, RangeInput, Slider) takes `size` (`sm`, `md` or `lg`, `md` by default; it replaces the native `size` attribute) and `invalid`, which draws the danger line and `aria-invalid="true"`: `TextInput(invalid={!valid()})`. RadioGroup, Segmented, ChipGroup, Rating, ColorInput, FileDrop, Checkbox and Toggle take `invalid` too. Checkbox and Toggle also take `name` and `issue`, showing the message of their field (or of the page) under the box as a FormField does. A FormField marks its control by itself while it shows a message.
- `Textarea(value={notes()} onInput={notes.set})`: the multi-line TextInput, taking the same props, `rows` among the rest, and working as a plain field without `value` and `onInput`. While focused it keeps the text as typed, and takes the parent's reading of it, such as without a blank line, once left. `writeFocused` lets any write land while focused, such as a chat clearing its draft after a send, the caret going to the end.
- `Select(value={kind()} options={[{ value: 'post', label: 'Post' }]} onChange={kind.set} emptyOption="Any")`:
  - `value` and `onChange` take the type of the options' values, so a union stays a union and a number option hands back a number: `const kind = signal<'post' | 'page'>('post')` passes `kind.set` with no cast.
  - `param="period"` (with `paramDefault`) holds a query param of the page with no island of the app's, as on Segmented: a pick runs `navigate({ query })`, keeping the other params and leaving the param out for `paramDefault` or the empty option, your `onChange` running first. Without a `value` the select shows the option the address names, else `paramDefault`, and follows each navigation and Back or Forward step. The select takes `param` as its name, so a GET form around it with a button works without JavaScript; alone it needs JavaScript.
  - `emptyOption` adds a first option worth `''`, labelled by the string, or blank with `true`; `value` and `onChange` then take `''` too, so hold a `signal<Kind | ''>`.
  - An option is `{ value, label, disabled? }`; `disabled` keeps it from being picked. A native option draws no icon, unlike the other groups' options.
  - `busy` shows a disabled row saying `loadingLabel` while the options are on their way.
  - `options` is an `OptionsSource` as on Combobox and MultiSelect: a list, a promise of one, or a function `({ q }) => list | promise`. It is asked in the browser with `query` (empty by default) and again whenever `query` changes, such as a search box beside the select (`Select(options={find} query={text()})`); the latest answer wins and replaces the options, so keep the picked option in it. `onLoad(options, query)` and `onError({ error, query })` follow each answer, with the arguments Combobox and MultiSelect pass.
  - The right option stays chosen when the options arrive after the value.
  - `submit` submits the form holding it once an option is picked, after `onChange`, for a GET filter form (see Forms). A pointer pick submits at once; a keyboard pick, reported by some browsers on each arrow key, waits for Enter or blur.

### DateInput, DateRangeInput, DateTimeInput, TimeInput

- `DateInput(aria-label="Due" name="due" value={due()} min="2026-01-01" max="2026-12-31" onChange={due.set})`: a native `input type="date"` holding `YYYY-MM-DD` (or `''` for none), so a form posts it with no JavaScript and the browser keeps its own keyboard entry. Once the island runs it adds a calendar button opening a popover in the page's locale, its weeks starting on the locale's first day or on `firstDay` (0 for Sunday to 6 for Saturday); days outside `min` and `max` are disabled. `open` shows the calendar from the page and `onOpenChange(open)` hears it shown and hidden, however it happened (the button, Escape, a click outside, a pick); `DateRangeInput` takes `onOpenChange(open, box)` with `'from'` or `'to'`. `disabled` disables the whole input and `disabledDay(iso)` closes single days (`isDisabled` before 1.89) (disabled, skipped by the arrows, a typed one flagged invalid and not reported), the `day(iso, state)` block draws the inside of a day button, and `onMonthChange(month)` hears the month on show as `YYYY-MM` (DateRangeInput: `(month, box)`).
  - Without `value` it keeps what is picked, for a form posting it by its `name`; with it, `onChange(value)` hears every change, typed, picked or reset by a form, and the field follows the parent.
  - Keyboard: the calendar opens on the chosen day (else today); the arrows move by day and week, Home and End to the ends of the week, PageUp and PageDown by month and with Shift by year, Enter or Space picks, Escape closes with focus back on the button. It is a labelled dialog holding a `grid` of day buttons named in full (`Wednesday 21 October 2026`), the month announced as it changes.
  - Other props are the input's own (`name`, `required`, `disabled`, `id`); `openLabel`, `previousLabel`, `nextLabel` and `todayLabel` replace the `ui.dateInput.*` messages.
- `DateRangeInput(label="Stay" fromName="from" toName="to" value={stay()} onChange={stay.set})`: two DateInputs for a `DateRange` (`[from, to]` of `YYYY-MM-DD` or `null`, open when empty), named `{label} from` and `{label} to` or by `fromLabel` and `toLabel`. `open` (`'from'`, `'to'` or `false`, controlled when given) shows one box's calendar and `onOpenChange(open, box)` hears either. `onChange` runs when a date is set and only when the range changed; a reversed pair is put back in order in both boxes. `dateRangeFrom(from, to)` reads the two posted strings the same way on the server.
- `DateTimeInput(aria-label="Starts" name="startsAt" value={startsAt()} min={now} onChange={startsAt.set})`: a native `input type="datetime-local"` that shows and takes its value on the visitor's clock, and once the island runs posts the instant under `name` with the visitor's offset (`2026-10-21T09:30:00+02:00`), which `s.Date()` reads right, so an app posts no `getTimezoneOffset()` field. `value`, `onChange` and the post hold that ISO text, or `''` for none; `value` may be written with any offset or `Z`. `min` and `max` are instants (ISO text or a `Date`), set on the box on the visitor's clock. The server cannot know the zone: it draws the box empty and with no `min` or `max`, a bare `YYYY-MM-DD` bound being that day's midnight, and without JavaScript the box posts its local text with no offset, which `s.Date()` refuses. A schema's `s.Date({ minimum })` is checked by the server only, as the posting field is hidden. Parts: `root` and `input`.
- `TimeInput(aria-label="Start" name="start" value="09:30" step={900} onChange={start.set})`: a native `input type="time"` holding `HH:MM` (`HH:MM:SS` under a minute's `step`), the same plain field without `value`. With `required`, `onChange` never hears `''`: the box goes back to the last time once left empty.

### RadioGroup

- `RadioGroup(options={[{ value: 'free', label: 'Free', description: 'Up to 3 sites' }, { value: 'pro', label: 'Pro' }]} value="free" name="plan" label="Plan" onChange={plan.set})`: a labelled list of real radios, so the arrow keys move between options and the group posts as `name` without JavaScript. Options are `{ value, label, description?, disabled? }`, values strings or numbers; a `description` sits under its label and is read after it. `value` starts the pick (`null` for none), an option may carry `value: null` for a "none" choice (it posts an empty string, so `kay check` fails it beside an option of `""`, and on a `value` that cannot be `null`, by the options' type: literal or union values), and a parent passing another follows it. `onChange` may return a promise, as Segmented's may: the group is busy until it settles and a refused change is put back. A form reset goes back to the start. `disabled` on the group disables every option. Use Segmented for a compact toggle row.
- `required` on RadioGroup, Segmented and Rating writes the native `required` on each radio and `aria-required` on the group, Checkbox and Toggle the native one, and ChipGroup `aria-required` on each box with the first refusing the submit (`requiredLabel`) while none is picked, which takes JavaScript. A FormField's `required` reaches a RadioGroup inside it only once the page runs, as the group is an island of its own: for a form that works without JavaScript, put `required` on the RadioGroup itself.
- `options` of RadioGroup, Segmented and ChipGroup is an `OptionsSource` like Select's: a list, a promise or a function returning one (`RadioGroup(options={() => loadPlans()} label="Plan")`). It is asked in the browser, the group is `aria-busy` with a `status` row saying `loadingLabel` (`ui.radioGroup.loading`, `ui.segmented.loading`, `ui.chipGroup.loading`) meanwhile and an alert row saying `failureLabel` with a `retryLabel` button (`ui.*.retry`, a function source only) when it rejects; `onLoad(options, query)` and `onError({ error, query })` follow the answer. Without a `value`, RadioGroup and Segmented start on the first option that is not disabled once the options arrive. New parts `status` and `retry`.
- A field error comes from the FormField around it: `FormField(label="Plan" name="plan" group)` shows the message refused for `plan` and marks each radio invalid. Leave `label` out there if the legend names the group; pass `aria-label` or `aria-labelledby` if a screen reader should hear the group named.

### RowsField

- `RowsField(rows={headers()} columns={[{ key: 'name', label: 'Header' }, { key: 'value', label: 'Value' }]} label="Response headers" addLabel="Add header" onChange={headers.set})`: the list of rows an app would otherwise hand-roll, a text box per column and an Add button, each row with a Remove button. Rows are plain objects of strings keyed by the columns' `key`; `onChange(rows)` hears every edit, added row and removed row, and the field follows the `rows` the parent passes. Other props: `placeholder` and `type` (`text`, `email`, `url`, `tel`, `search`) on a column, `min` and `max` rows (Remove and Add stop there), `removeLabel={index => 'Remove header ' + (index + 1)}`. A `number` column holds the figure as text; a column marked `cell: true` is drawn by the `@cell(column, row, index, set)` block (`set(text)` writes the row; `column.name` is the posting name), so a Select, checkbox or Combobox takes part in rows, posting and reset; `onAdd(row, index)` and `onRemove(row, index)` hear the add and remove. A cell is drawn once per row and keeps its control and focus while the row is edited, so read `row` where it is used (`value={row.unit}`), not once at the top of the block.
- `rowIssues={index => problems[index]}` (or a record by place) marks one row: its text boxes take `aria-invalid` and the danger colour, and the message shows under the row (`issue` part), linked by `aria-describedby`. `invalid` still marks the whole field; a cell gets it as the block's fifth argument, `issue` (`invalid`, `message`, `id`): `Select(invalid={issue.invalid} aria-describedby={issue.id})`.
- Inside a Form, FormDialog or SaveForm an action's refusal reaches the rows with no `rowIssues`: a handler's `errors` keyed `<name>_<key>.<n>` or `<name>_<key>[n]` mark that cell of row n (from 0) and show under that row, `<name>.<n>` or `<name>[n]` marks the row, and a schema refusal of an array column, keyed by the bare `<name>_<key>`, marks the column and shows under the first row. None of them is toasted. `size` (`sm`, `md`, `lg`) goes to every box and to a cell block as `column.size`. A FormField around a list control (MultiSelect, a Combobox picking several) shows a refusal of one item, keyed `<name>.<n>` or `<name>[n]`, as its own.
- Every box is named for screen readers by its column and row (`Header 2`), the headings sit once above the rows, a removed row leaves the boxes of the others as they are, and Add focuses the new row's first box. Adding and removing are announced (`ui.rowsField.*`).
- In a form posting to an action, `name="header"` makes each column post under `header_<key>` (or the column's own `name`), once per row in order and blank cells kept, so the action reads `header_name: s.Array(s.String())` and `header_value: s.Array(s.String())` of one length. Without `onChange` the field keeps its rows itself; a form reset and a SaveForm's Discard bring back the rows it started with (the saved ones after a save), and an added or removed row counts as an unsaved change.
