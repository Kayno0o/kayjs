# kayjs-ui: Inputs, selects, editors and forms

## Common tasks

### TextInput, Textarea, Select

- `TextInput(value={title()} onInput={title.set} onChange={save})`:
  - `onInput(value)` runs on each keystroke, and `onChange(value)` once the value is committed, on blur or Enter. `onBlur(event)` gets the box's own blur, as on an `input`.
  - `type="number"` hands back a number, or `null` once emptied, to both callbacks, and takes a number, `null` or a string as `value`: `TextInput(type="number" value={count()} onInput={count.set})` with `signal<number | null>(1)`. A box partway through a number, such as `1,`, reports nothing until it parses. A parent writing back its own spelling of the same number leaves the caret alone.
  - Without `value` and `onInput` it is a plain field keeping what is typed, for a form posting to an action without JavaScript: `TextInput(name="email" type="email" required autocomplete="email")`, with `value` to start it filled. kay refills only the fields a form's own template writes, so pass `value={failure?.values.email}` to keep what a refused post sent.
- Every field control (TextInput, Textarea, Select, Combobox, MultiSelect, DateInput, DateRangeInput, DateTimeInput, TimeInput, RangeInput, Slider) takes `size` (`sm`, `md` or `lg`, `md` by default; it replaces the native `size` attribute) and `invalid`, which draws the danger line and `aria-invalid="true"`: `TextInput(invalid={!valid()})`. A FormField marks its control by itself while it shows a message.
- `Textarea(value={notes()} onInput={notes.set})`: the multi-line TextInput, taking the same props, `rows` among the rest, and working as a plain field without `value` and `onInput`. While focused it keeps the text as typed, and takes the parent's reading of it, such as without a blank line, once left. `writeFocused` lets any write land while focused, such as a chat clearing its draft after a send, the caret going to the end.
- `Select(value={kind()} options={[{ value: 'post', label: 'Post' }]} onChange={kind.set} emptyOption="Any")`:
  - `value` and `onChange` take the type of the options' values, so a union stays a union and a number option hands back a number: `const kind = signal<'post' | 'page'>('post')` passes `kind.set` with no cast.
  - `param="period"` (with `paramDefault`) holds a query param of the page with no island of the app's, as on Segmented: a pick runs `navigate({ query })`, keeping the other params and leaving the param out for `paramDefault` or the empty option, your `onChange` running first. The select takes `param` as its name, so a GET form around it with a button works without JavaScript; alone it needs JavaScript.
  - `emptyOption` adds a first option worth `''`, labelled by the string, or blank with `true`; `value` and `onChange` then take `''` too, so hold a `signal<Kind | ''>`.
  - An option is `{ value, label, disabled? }`; `disabled` keeps it from being picked. A native option draws no icon, unlike the other groups' options.
  - `busy` shows a disabled row saying `loadingLabel` while the options are on their way.
  - The right option stays chosen when the options arrive after the value.
  - `submit` submits the form holding it once an option is picked, after `onChange`, for a GET filter form (see Forms). A pointer pick submits at once; a keyboard pick, reported by some browsers on each arrow key, waits for Enter or blur.

### DateInput, DateRangeInput, DateTimeInput, TimeInput

- `DateInput(aria-label="Due" name="due" value={due()} min="2026-01-01" max="2026-12-31" onChange={due.set})`: a native `input type="date"` holding `YYYY-MM-DD` (or `''` for none), so a form posts it with no JavaScript and the browser keeps its own keyboard entry. Once the island runs it adds a calendar button opening a popover in the page's locale, its weeks starting on the locale's first day or on `firstDay` (0 for Sunday to 6 for Saturday); days outside `min` and `max` are disabled. `open` shows the calendar from the page and `onOpenChange(open)` hears it shown and hidden, however it happened (the button, Escape, a click outside, a pick); `DateRangeInput` takes `onOpenChange(open, box)` with `'from'` or `'to'`. `isDisabled(iso)` closes single days (disabled, skipped by the arrows, a typed one flagged invalid and not reported), the `day(iso, state)` block draws the inside of a day button, and `onMonthChange(month)` hears the month on show as `YYYY-MM` (DateRangeInput: `(month, box)`).
  - Without `value` it keeps what is picked, for a form posting it by its `name`; with it, `onChange(value)` hears every change, typed, picked or reset by a form, and the field follows the parent.
  - Keyboard: the calendar opens on the chosen day (else today); the arrows move by day and week, Home and End to the ends of the week, PageUp and PageDown by month and with Shift by year, Enter or Space picks, Escape closes with focus back on the button. It is a labelled dialog holding a `grid` of day buttons named in full (`Wednesday 21 October 2026`), the month announced as it changes.
  - Other props are the input's own (`name`, `required`, `disabled`, `id`); `openLabel`, `previousLabel`, `nextLabel` and `todayLabel` replace the `ui.dateInput.*` messages.
- `DateRangeInput(label="Stay" fromName="from" toName="to" value={stay()} onChange={stay.set})`: two DateInputs for a `DateRange` (`[from, to]` of `YYYY-MM-DD` or `null`, open when empty), named `{label} from` and `{label} to` or by `fromLabel` and `toLabel`. `open` (`'from'`, `'to'` or `false`, controlled when given) shows one box's calendar and `onOpenChange(open, box)` hears either. `onChange` runs when a date is set and only when the range changed; a reversed pair is put back in order in both boxes. `dateRangeFrom(from, to)` reads the two posted strings the same way on the server.
- `DateTimeInput(aria-label="Starts" name="startsAt" value={startsAt()} min={now} onChange={startsAt.set})`: a native `input type="datetime-local"` that shows and takes its value on the visitor's clock, and once the island runs posts the instant under `name` with the visitor's offset (`2026-10-21T09:30:00+02:00`), which `s.Date()` reads right, so an app posts no `getTimezoneOffset()` field. `value`, `onChange` and the post hold that ISO text, or `''` for none; `value` may be written with any offset or `Z`. `min` and `max` are instants (ISO text or a `Date`), set on the box on the visitor's clock. The server cannot know the zone: it draws the box empty and with no `min` or `max`, a bare `YYYY-MM-DD` bound being that day's midnight, and without JavaScript the box posts its local text with no offset, which `s.Date()` refuses. A schema's `s.Date({ minimum })` is checked by the server only, as the posting field is hidden. Parts: `root` and `input`.
- `TimeInput(aria-label="Start" name="start" value="09:30" step={900} onChange={start.set})`: a native `input type="time"` holding `HH:MM` (`HH:MM:SS` under a minute's `step`), the same plain field without `value`. With `required`, `onChange` never hears `''`: the box goes back to the last time once left empty.

### RangeInput, Slider, MultiSelect, Combobox, FileDrop, Menu, IconPicker

- `RangeInput(label="Hours" min={0} max={24} unit="h" value={[2, null]} onChange={hours.set})`: two number boxes for a range, each bound open when empty (`null`). `onChange([from, to])` runs once a box is committed, on blur or Enter, and only when the range changed; a reversed pair is put back in order in the boxes too. Each box is named `{label} from` and `{label} to`, or `fromLabel` and `toLabel`. `rangeFrom(low, high)` reads two strings the same way, for a server reading the posted boxes.
- `Slider(label="Delay" min={0} max={500} step={10} value={delay()} formatValue={ms => `${ms} ms`} name="delay" onInput={delay.set} onChange={save})`: one value on a native `input type="range"`, the label and the value written above the track, drawn from the theme (accent thumb, field-border track) so an app styles no `::-webkit-slider-thumb`. Pick Slider for one number, RangeInput for a from/to pair. `onInput(number)` runs on every move of the thumb and after a form reset with the value it went back to. `onChange(number)` runs once with the value let go of, as the native `change` does: on pointer release, on each key press (arrows, Home, End, PageUp, PageDown) and after a reset that moved the thumb. Like Toggle's, a promise it returns holds the slider busy (`aria-busy`, no new gesture) and `false`, a rejection or a throw (logged) puts the thumb back on the last accepted value, telling `onInput` it; use it instead of debouncing `onInput` to send a value. `showValue={false}` hides the written value, `formatValue` words it and the `aria-valuetext`. A `value` the parent passes later is followed, one it moves onto the value being changed while a promise is pending (from `onChange` too) is kept even when that change is refused, an `onInput` echo before the release not counting, the thumb going back; an `aria-label` names the input in place of `label`, and `name`, `id`, `disabled` and the other input attributes go on the input, which posts its number in a plain form.
- `ColorInput(label="Light" name="light" value={light()} onChange={light.set})`: a native `input type="color"` drawn as a swatch in the theme, the label and the `#rrggbb` written beside it; it posts under `name` in a plain form. `onChange(value)` hears the colour once the picker commits it (not each shade dragged over), a form reset that moved it included. A promise holds the input busy until it settles (a click opens no picker meanwhile), and the previous colour is put back when it rejects (logged) or resolves to false, so a slow bulb never shows a colour it refused. A `value` the parent passes later is followed, one moved onto the colour being changed while a promise is pending (from `onChange` too) is kept even when that change is refused, and one that is not `#rrggbb` shows as black. An `aria-label` or `aria-labelledby` passed to it names the input in place of `label`.
- `MultiSelect(label="Planets" options={planets} value={['mars']} name="planet" onChange={picked.set})`:
  - A trigger summing up what is picked (`placeholder`, `Any` by default, the one label, or "2 selected", worded by `selectedLabel(count)`) opens a popover of checkboxes with All and None, a group named by `label`. Screen readers hear the trigger as `{label}: {summary}`, a count described by the labels it stands for, and `emptyLabel` from a status region once the search hides every option.
  - `open` shows the panel from the page and `onOpenChange(open)` hears every change, a click outside and Escape included: a page driving `open` sets its own value back there.
  - Past eight options, or with `search`, the panel opens on a search box filtering the options, focused; otherwise the first checkbox takes focus. ArrowDown reaches the options, where the arrows, Home and End move between them, a tint and an accent bar marking the one the keyboard is on; Tab past the last control closes the panel, and Escape closes it with focus back on the trigger.
  - An option is `{ value, label, icon?, disabled? }` as in the other groups: `icon` sits before the label, and a `disabled` option cannot be flipped and stays as it is under All and None.
  - What is picked stays in the options' order, an array of the options' own value type, so a union-typed list needs no cast. `name` posts each picked value as a hidden field, so the control submits inside a plain form.
- `FileDrop(accept="image/*" multiple paste onFiles={upload})` with a `@content(zone)` block:
  - Files dropped on the zone, picked through `zone.open()` or, with `paste`, pasted anywhere on the page outside a text field, reach `onFiles(files)`: those `accept` allows, one unless `multiple`, never an empty list. A file the browser gives no type, such as a `.csv` on Windows, passes a type in `accept`, for its receiver to judge by its name. `onReject(files)` gets the dropped or picked files left out (not pasted ones), those `accept` refuses and, without `multiple`, the matching ones beyond the first, to say why they were ignored.
  - `zone.dragging()` reads whether files hover the zone, which also carries `data-dragging`.
  - `size="sm"` (`md` by default) draws it as a slim row, its content from the left, to sit inline in a form rather than as a large target.
- `Menu(label="Actions" icon="plus" items={[{ label: 'Rename', onSelect: rename }, 'separator', { label: 'Delete', icon: 'delete', tone: 'danger', onSelect: remove }]})`:
  - An item's `tone` is any `Tone`.
  - An item's old `danger: true` flag is deprecated for `tone: 'danger'`, which wins when both are given, and goes in 2.0.0.
  - `data-*`, `aria-*` and `style` go on the icon button, `open` and `onOpenChange` are Popover's.
  - `@trigger(attributes)` replaces the icon button, for a labelled Button or a user name with an avatar: `Menu(label="Actions" items={items})` then `@trigger(attributes)` and `Button({...attributes} label="Actions")`. The attributes carry `popovertarget`, `aria-expanded`, `aria-haspopup="menu"` and the anchor style (`MenuTriggerProps`), and `icon`, `size`, `variant` and `tone` then style nothing. AppSwitcher takes the same block.
  - An IconButton opening a `role="menu"` popover; `'separator'` draws a rule between groups, and `class` styles that button.
  - Opening focuses the first enabled entry; the arrows, Home and End move between enabled entries, an accent bar marking the one the keyboard is on, and Tab closes it.
  - `at={{ x, y }}` with `open` and `onOpenChange` opens it at a point of the window (`clientX`, `clientY` of a click) with no trigger and no `icon`, as a context menu or a choice next to a click; `placement` is `bottom-start` then, the browser flips it inside the window, Escape and a click outside close it and give focus back.
  - An entry with `href` (`{ label: 'Open', href: '/x' }`, `onSelect` then optional) is an `a role="menuitem"`: middle-click, Ctrl+click, "copy link" and the status bar work, Enter and Space follow it, and the menu closes as it is followed (`onSelect`, when given, runs then). An absolute address (`https://...`) opens in a new tab with `rel="noopener noreferrer"`; a `disabled` entry stays a disabled button.
  - An entry's `onSelect` runs once the menu has closed and focus is back on the trigger, so a dialog it opens takes focus from there.
- `IconPicker(search={findIcons} value={post.icon} name="icon" onChange={icon.set})`:
  - A search that rejects is logged and shown as `errorLabel` (`ui.iconPicker.error`), the last grid kept. `onSearch(query)` and `onError(error)` hear the searches.
  - A search box over a grid of the icons `search(query, limit, offset)` returns, from the `offset`th on, as `IconChoice`s, `{ name, icon }` with `icon` the IconProps to draw: typically an action, since a whole icon set is too large to ship, as `search={(query, limit, offset) => findIcons({ query, limit, offset })}`. The app's action builds them on the server with kay's `iconNames` and `iconProps` over the set's JSON, so the set never ships to the browser:

    ```ts
    import tabler from '@iconify-json/tabler/icons.json'
    import { s } from 'kay'
    import { iconNames, iconProps } from 'kay/icons'

    export const findIcons = action(s.Object({ query: s.String({ maxLength: 200 }), limit: s.Integer(), offset: s.Integer() }), async ({ query, limit, offset }) =>
      iconNames(tabler).filter(name => name.includes(query)).slice(offset, offset + limit).map(name => ({ name: `tabler:${name}`, icon: iconProps(tabler, name)! })))
    ```

  - The search runs 200ms after the typing pauses, from 50 icons; `Show more` asks for the next 50, at `offset` 50, 100 and so on, once a page comes back full, and adds them to the grid. A page with no icon the grid lacks, as an app ignoring `offset` gives, ends the paging. The last grid stays while a search is in flight, and a search a later one overtook is dropped.
  - Picking the chosen icon again, or the selection's clear button, clears it: `onChange` gets the name, or an empty string. The tooltip and label read the name without its `prefix:`.
  - `name` submits the chosen name as a hidden field, which fires a bubbling `input` event on each pick. Enter in the search box never submits the form.
  - The selection draws the chosen icon once a search found it; a `value` no search returned yet shows its name alone.
- MultiSelect and Menu draw their popover's id as Popover does, or take an `id`. RangeInput and MultiSelect take data alone, so a static page can hold them; FileDrop, Menu and IconPicker take functions, so they render inside an island.

- `Combobox(label="Planet" options={planets} value={planet()} onChange={planet.set} name="planet" create)`: a text input with a listbox, filtered from the list as the visitor types. `open` shows the list from the page and `onOpenChange(open)` hears it open and close (focus, typing, Escape, a pick, focus leaving), as Popover's does.
  - `options` is a list of `{ value, label, description?, disabled? }`, or a function handed `{ q }` once `minLength` (1) characters are typed, after a 200 ms pause, answering the options: an action, or an API call in a function. Only the latest answer lands; a failed call says `failureLabel`. `onChange(value, option)` runs on a pick, and with `''` when typing drops it. `onInput(text)`, `onLoad(options, query)` and `onError(error, query)` hear the text and loads.
  - A `description` is a quieter second line under the label: plain data, so an action can answer it. The input shows and the filter matches the label alone, the row is named by its label and read the description after (`aria-describedby`). `@option(option)` draws each row yourself instead (`Combobox(...)` then `@option(commit)` and its markup); a block is a function, so the Combobox renders inside an island (`// kay-island`). The row is named by the option's label through `aria-label`, so the rest of the block is not read: add your own `aria-describedby` inside it. Parts: `optionLabel`, `optionDescription` beside `option`.
  - `valueLabel` is the text of `value` for a remote list that has not returned it: the input shows it and no load is made on focus, and without it a remote `value` leaves the input empty (a fixed list shows the value). Inside a FormField the label's text alone names the input.
  - `create` offers the typed text as a last row (`createLabel(text)`) unless an option bears it; picking it hands the text as the value and to `onCreate(text)`.
  - `name` submits the value picked, else the text typed; a label typed whole counts as a pick, and the Enter adopting it does not submit the form. Without JavaScript it is a text field posting its text, a list offered as the browser's own suggestions. A form reset puts back the text and pick.
  - The combobox pattern: the input stays focused, ArrowDown and ArrowUp open and move through the rows (disabled ones skipped, wrapping), Alt+ArrowDown opens, Enter picks, Escape closes (an open list with no rows too) then clears; a status line reads the count, `loadingLabel`, `emptyLabel` and `failureLabel`. Parts: `root`, `input`, `list`, `option`, `status`.

### RadioGroup

- `RadioGroup(options={[{ value: 'free', label: 'Free', description: 'Up to 3 sites' }, { value: 'pro', label: 'Pro' }]} value="free" name="plan" label="Plan" onChange={plan.set})`: a labelled list of real radios, so the arrow keys move between options and the group posts as `name` without JavaScript. Options are `{ value, label, description?, disabled? }`, values strings or numbers; a `description` sits under its label and is read after it. `value` starts the pick (`null` for none), an option may carry `value: null` for a "none" choice (it posts an empty string, so `kay check` fails it beside an option of `""`, and on a `value` that cannot be `null`, by the options' type: literal or union values), and a parent passing another follows it. `onChange` may return a promise, as Segmented's may: the group is busy until it settles and a refused change is put back. A form reset goes back to the start. `disabled` on the group disables every option. Use Segmented for a compact toggle row.
- A field error comes from the FormField around it: `FormField(label="Plan" name="plan" group)` shows the message refused for `plan` and marks each radio invalid. Leave `label` out there if the legend names the group; pass `aria-label` or `aria-labelledby` if a screen reader should hear the group named.

### RowsField

- `RowsField(rows={headers()} columns={[{ key: 'name', label: 'Header' }, { key: 'value', label: 'Value' }]} label="Response headers" addLabel="Add header" onChange={headers.set})`: the list of rows an app would otherwise hand-roll, a text box per column and an Add button, each row with a Remove button. Rows are plain objects of strings keyed by the columns' `key`; `onChange(rows)` hears every edit, added row and removed row, and the field follows the `rows` the parent passes. Other props: `placeholder` and `type` (`text`, `email`, `url`, `tel`, `search`) on a column, `min` and `max` rows (Remove and Add stop there), `removeLabel={index => 'Remove header ' + (index + 1)}`. A `number` column holds the figure as text; a column marked `cell: true` is drawn by the `@cell(column, row, index, set)` block (`set(text)` writes the row; `column.name` is the posting name), so a Select, checkbox or Combobox takes part in rows, posting and reset; `onAdd(row, index)` and `onRemove(row, index)` hear the add and remove.
- Every box is named for screen readers by its column and row (`Header 2`), the headings sit once above the rows, a removed row leaves the boxes of the others as they are, and Add focuses the new row's first box. Adding and removing are announced (`ui.rowsField.*`).
- In a form posting to an action, `name="header"` makes each column post under `header_<key>` (or the column's own `name`), once per row in order and blank cells kept, so the action reads `header_name: s.Array(s.String())` and `header_value: s.Array(s.String())` of one length. Without `onChange` the field keeps its rows itself; a form reset and a SaveForm's Discard bring back the rows it started with (the saved ones after a save), and an added or removed row counts as an unsaved change.
