# kayjs-auth: Events, the audit log, admin and organizations

## Events and the audit log

`onAuthEvent(info)` hears every security mail's event above, mailed or not, and these, never mailed. `info` is one object, `{ event, user, context, ...fields }`: `context` is `{ ip, userAgent, actor }`, and the fields are the event's own, as below. Switch on `info.event` and the fields are typed. A failed password sign-in is reported after the response, so a hook or an audit read right after the call may not see it yet.

- `signed-in`, `method` how: `password`, `register`, `reset-password`, `magic-link`, `email-code`, `oauth` with the `provider`, `passkey`, `guest`, or `app` for `auth.signIn`. A sign-in through the second factor is heard once it is answered, with its first factor.
- `signed-out`.
- `sign-in-failed`, for an account that exists only: `method` `password`, `two-factor` or `passkey`.
- An admin's doing, `actor` the admin: `role-granted` and `role-revoked`, `role`; `banned`, `reason` when given; `unbanned`; `impersonation-started` and `impersonation-ended`.
- In an organization, `organizationId`: `organization-created` and `organization-deleted`; `member-joined` and `member-roles-changed`, with the `roles` given; `member-left`; `member-removed`. A manager's doing names them as `actor`.
- `oauth-client-approved` (first approval of an app, or more scopes) and `oauth-client-revoked`, `client` the app's name.

`onEvent(event, user, detail, context)` is the form before 1.52, with the parts of the fields packed in a string (`oauth:<provider>`, `<orgId> <role> <role>`): it still works for now, logs a warning, and `kay check` reports it as `deprecated-auth-event`. The audit log keeps the same string as an entry's `detail`.

`context` is `{ ip, userAgent, actor }`, `actor` the user who acted when it was not the one it happened to, such as an admin. While an admin acts as a user, every event names the admin as `actor`, in the audit log too: an organization created or a member removed, a sign-out everywhere.

With `audit: true`, or `{ maxAge }` as seconds or a string such as `'90d'` (a year by default), the same events are kept in the store's audit log too, pruned of older ones at most hourly. `auditLog({ before, limit })` gives the signed-in user their own, newest first (entries of one millisecond newest written first, as their ids are time-ordered; entries from before that sort by random id): `{ id, userId, event, detail, ip, userAgent, actorId, at }`. Pass the last entry of a page as `before` for the next one, which skips and repeats none, as is or after a JSON hop; a bare `Date` (or its ISO text) excludes that whole millisecond, and a cursor that is no date answers an empty page. The log is kept past a user's deletion, so it still says what happened, but nothing in it leads back to them: their entries take a new random `userId` and lose the address, browser and detail (which can hold an address or a passkey's name), and the entries they acted in, as an admin or a manager, take it as `actorId` and lose the address and browser. An entry is kept `maxAge`, a year by default, for the account's security and the admins' accountability. Writing it never fails what it records: a failure is logged.

`describeAuditEntry(entry, { locale, wording })` words an entry for a person, its detail included (`Role editor granted`, `Signed in with discord`), in English or French as the pages are, English for another locale. `wording` takes the app's own phrase for any event, a string or a function of the detail, over kayjs-auth's; `auditWording` holds kayjs-auth's by language.

## Admin

`admin: 'admin'`, any requirement a guard takes, names who may manage users; without it, and to anyone else, to an admin acting as someone or to a bearer token, an admin's included, every method below answers `{ ok: false, error: 'forbidden' }`. The methods changing a user, `grantRole`, `revokeRole`, `banUser`, `unbanUser` and `deleteUser`, also answer `stale` unless the admin signed in or re-proved (`reprove`) in the last ten minutes, so a session left open or stolen changes no one; reading users and the audit log takes no such proof.

- `listUsers({ search, offset, limit })` gives `{ ok: true, users, total }`, newest first, 50 a page by default and 200 at most; `search` matches part of an email or a username whatever its case, or a whole id. `getUser(id)` gives one. Each is a `User` with `createdAt` and `ban`, `{ at, until, reason }` while banned, else null.
- `grantRole(id, role)` and `revokeRole(id, role)` take a role the `roles` option declares (`unknown-role` otherwise), effective on the user's next request. An admin taking back a role of their own that leaves them no longer an admin is refused (`self`), and when two admins revoke each other at once at least one stays an admin: a revoke that would have left none is undone and answers `last-admin`.
- `banUser(id, { reason, until })` bars the user from signing in, for good or until that date (`until` in the past answers `until`), and signs them out everywhere, bearer tokens revoked. Every sign-in of theirs answers `{ ok: false, error: 'banned' }`, an OAuth one `?error=banned`, and `auth.signIn` throws. An admin cannot ban themselves (`self`) or another admin (`admin`): revoke the role first. `unbanUser(id)` lifts it.
- `deleteUser(id)` deletes the user as `deleteAccount` does, `onDelete(user)` first, and mails them the `account-deleted` notice when `email` is set and their address is verified. The audit log keeps an `account-deleted` entry with the admin as `actorId`, and `onAuthEvent` gets the admin as `context.actor`. It gives `{ ok: true }`, or `{ ok: false, error }` with `forbidden`, `stale`, `not-found`, `self` or `admin` as for `banUser`, or `last-owner` while the user is the only owner of an organization with other members (an organization they are alone in is deleted with them).
- `impersonate(id)` answers `stale` unless the admin signed in or re-proved within ten minutes, as the other admin changes do, and signs the admin's browser in as the user, keeping the admin's own sign-in; `impersonator()` gives the admin, to show a banner, and `stopImpersonating()` signs them back in as themselves, or out when their own sign-in ended meanwhile. Meanwhile nothing taking a proof passes (`reprove` answers `impersonating`, and the sign-in counts as stale), no new sign-in mail goes out, and the impersonation ends once the admin is signed out everywhere, banned or no longer an admin. Another admin, oneself and a banned user cannot be impersonated. `signOut()` ends both sign-ins.
- `adminAuditLog({ userId, before, limit })` gives `{ ok: true, entries }`, everyone's audit log or one user's, paged by `before` as `auditLog` is.

## Organizations

Teams or workspaces users belong to, with roles of their own in each:

```ts
createAuth({
  // ...
  organizations: {
    roles: { owner: ['admin'], admin: ['member'], member: [] },
    owner: 'owner',
    manage: 'admin',
    defaultRoles: ['member'],
    invitationPage: '/invitation',
  },
})
```

- `roles` is a hierarchy as the site's `roles` is, typed the same way. Whoever creates an organization holds `owner`: owners alone delete it, and the last one cannot leave, be removed, lose the role or delete their account (`last-owner`); a sole owner who is also the only member deletes the organization with the account. `deletionImpact()` gives `{ deleted, blocked }`, the organization names the signed-in user's deletion would delete or be refused for, and the account page shows both before the proof. A deletion not refused (a stale guest, an unverified registration replaced) hands such an organization to its longest-standing member, and owners leaving, demoted or deleted at once cannot leave it ownerless: a write that would have left none is undone and answers `last-owner`. `manage`, `owner` by default, is what renaming, inviting, removing members and changing their roles take. `create`, any requirement a guard takes, limits who may create one; anyone signed in by default.
- `createOrganization({ name, slug? })` answers `{ ok: true, membership }`, or `invalid-name` (1 to 100 characters, with no control characters, line breaks or direction marks, the name going into the invitation mail), `invalid-slug` (lowercase letters, digits and dashes, 64 at most, lowercased first) or `slug-taken`. A membership is `{ organization: { id, name, slug, createdAt }, roles, joinedAt }`.
- `organizations()` lists the user's memberships; `organization(ref)` gives one, by id or slug, null unless a member; `isMember(ref, role?)` says whether they are one, holding `role` when given. `updateOrganization(ref, { name, slug })` renames it, `slug: null` clearing the slug, and `deleteOrganization(ref)` deletes it with its memberships and invitations, answering `'impersonating'` to an admin acting as the owner, as it cannot be undone.
- `members(ref)` gives `{ ok: true, members }`, each `{ user: { id, email, username }, roles, joinedAt }`, for any member to see. `setMemberRoles(ref, userId, roles)` replaces a member's roles, `removeMember(ref, userId)` removes one, and `leaveOrganization(ref)` leaves. A manager gives only roles they hold, to a member holding none above theirs: an admin cannot make an owner nor demote or remove one (`forbidden`).
- `invite(ref, email, roles?)` mails the address a link to `invitationPage` + `?invitation=<id>` (`unavailable` without it, or without a mailer, or when the mailer throws), giving `roles`, `defaultRoles` without them, once accepted: `unverified` while the manager has not verified their own address, `member` when the address belongs to a member already, `limited` past `invite`, 30 an hour per manager, or `inviteAddress`, 5 a day per invited address whoever invites it, a count a stranger inviting the address can use up. Inviting an address again replaces its invitation. `invitations(ref)` lists the open ones for managers and `revokeInvitation(ref, id)` revokes one. An invitation lasts `invitationMaxAge`, seconds or a string such as `'7d'`, a week by default. The mail's `kind` is `'invitation'`, the organization's name its template's `detail`.
- The page reads `invitation(id)`, `{ id, organization, email, roles, invitedBy, createdAt, expiresAt }` or null once revoked, accepted or expired, and posts to an action calling `acceptInvitation(id)`: only an account verified at the invited address joins (`wrong-account`, `unverified`), which an invitee without an account reaches by registering with it. `myInvitations()` lists those open to the user's verified address, `declineInvitation(id)` turns one down.
- `deleteOrganization`, `setMemberRoles`, `removeMember` and `leaveOrganization` need a session: a bearer or OAuth token answers `signed-out`, so a leaked API key cannot delete an organization or change its roles. The other organization calls, reading and inviting included, accept a token as its user.
- Any failure above may also be `signed-out`, `not-found` (no such organization, or one the user is no member of, so its existence does not leak) or `forbidden` (a member whose roles do not reach).
- `auth.memberOf(role?)` is a requirement met by a member of the organization the route's `org` param names, by id or slug, holding `role` there when given: `src/routes/orgs/[org]/_guard.ts` exporting `auth.guard({ redirect: '/login', require: auth.memberOf('admin') })`, or `.get('/orgs/:org/settings', handler, { auth: auth.memberOf('admin') })` behind a gate. `organizations.param`, or `memberOf(role, { param })`, names another param. A guard checks a page's actions against the params of the page they are posted from, so an action scopes what it touches by `ctx.params.org`, never by its input. A route without the param, or kay before 1.23, whose guards get no params, is refused, never let through.
- Deleting a user deletes their memberships; an organization whose last owner deletes their account stays without one. `exportData()` lists the user's memberships as `organizations`.
